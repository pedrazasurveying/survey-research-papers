SECTION 1: SUMMARY OF WHAT EXISTS
================================================================================

WORKFORCE AGING AND CONTRACTION (Queries 1-6)
----------------------------------------------

The literature on surveying workforce aging and contraction is SPARSE in peer-
reviewed venues but RICH in trade/industry sources. Key findings:

PEER-REVIEWED SOURCES (very few):
- Wiejak-Roy & Edwards (2025) in Survey Review: Regulatory comparison US/UK.
  Only peer-reviewed paper found specifically on surveying workforce/licensure.
- Heydari et al. (2024) in JME: Systematic review on DEI in AEC industry.
  Covers construction broadly but not surveying as a distinct subfield.
- Bezdek (2009) in ES&T: Geoscience workforce gap projection.
  Surveying is a small fraction of the geoscience umbrella.

GOVERNMENT/INSTITUTIONAL DATA:
- BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook (2025): Projects 4% growth for surveyors,
  5% for surveying technicians 2024-2034. ~3,900 annual openings, mostly
  replacement. Median wage $72,740.
- BLS CPS Table 11: 82.8% male, 83.1% White, 3.55% Black (2023 data).
  Combines surveyors with cartographers and photogrammetrists.
- NCEES Squared (2024): PS exam 56% first-time pass rate, FS exam ~46%.
  Low candidate volumes (hundreds). No demographic breakdown of candidates.
- AGI Geoscience Workforce Projections (2020): 27% workforce loss by 2029
  due to retirements. Covers geosciences broadly.
- NSPS Demographics Survey (~2020): 2,409 respondents. 94.3% male, 92.3%
  White, 0.3% Black. Average age ~58. Critical primary data source.

TRADE AND INDUSTRY SOURCES (most abundant):
- Multiple trade publications document the crisis consistently: average age
  57-60, 2-3 retiring for every 1 entering, only 14% under age 34, only
  ~26 ABET programs remain, several recently closed.
- SAM apprenticeship program (DOL-certified), NSPS Future of Surveying
  Initiative, NYSAPLS workforce development efforts documented.
- American Surveyor policy commentary (March 2026) represents most current
  trade coverage.

CROSS-INDUSTRY COMPARISONS (Queries 7-11)
------------------------------------------

Adjacent fields have SUBSTANTIALLY more peer-reviewed research on workforce
diversity and pipeline issues:

CIVIL ENGINEERING / CONSTRUCTION:
- ASCE JME published 23+ papers in a 2022 special collection on DEI alone.
- ASCE JPIEP published a 2018 special issue on diversity in civil/environmental
  engineering.
- Systematic literature reviews exist (Heydari et al. 2024).
- Quantified: <15% women, <20% nonwhite in civil engineering; 3.9% women in
  executive engineering roles; 92% of construction firms report hiring difficulty.
- Harvard JCHS, McKinsey, AGC/NCCER provide regular data.

TRADES (PLUMBING/ELECTRICAL):
- DOL apprenticeship data provides baseline diversity metrics.
- UA Diversity Pipeline Program (1,200 youth trained), PHCC Equity Hiring
  Toolkit (18% minority hire increase) documented.
- Women 2.1% of plumbers, 9% of all apprentices. 80% of tradeswomen report
  harassment. Pre-apprenticeship programs targeting underrepresented groups
  showing early promise.

GIS/REMOTE SENSING:
- Limited demographic data specific to GIS workforce found.
- Esri and GIM International discuss skills mismatch and demand growth.
- Market growing ($90B to $263B projected) but workforce demographics not
  well-studied.

ARCHITECTURE:
- BEST demographic data infrastructure of any comparison field.
- NCARB publishes annual "By the Numbers" with detailed race/gender breakdowns.
- NCARB/NOMA joint survey specifically studies minority licensure experience.
- AIA published Workplace Experiences Survey (n=1,346).
- Shows clear progress trajectory: 34% of new architects are people of color
  (up from 25% in 2019), but Black architects still only 2% of practitioners.

SECTION 2: CRITICAL GAPS IDENTIFIED
================================================================================

GAP 1 (SEVERE): NO PEER-REVIEWED LONGITUDINAL STUDY OF SURVEYING WORKFORCE
DEMOGRAPHICS
- No academic study tracks the size, age distribution, racial composition,
  or gender breakdown of the licensed surveying workforce over time.
- The NSPS demographics survey is the closest thing to primary data, but it
  is a one-time self-selected sample with no peer-reviewed publication.
- CONTRAST: Architecture has NCARB annual data; geosciences have AGI biennial
  reports; civil engineering has multiple JME studies.

GAP 2 (SEVERE): NO STATE-BY-STATE LICENSURE TREND DATA IN THE LITERATURE
- Anecdotal reports of licensure decline are abundant, but no published study
  compiles PLS/RPLS licensure numbers across states over time.
- NCEES publishes exam statistics but not demographic breakdowns of candidates
  or pass rates by race/gender.
- Individual state boards (e.g., TBPELS in Texas) have records but no
  aggregated analysis has been published.

GAP 3 (MAJOR): NO EMPIRICAL STUDY OF DIVERSITY/INCLUSION SPECIFICALLY IN
LAND SURVEYING
- The closest studies address AEC or construction broadly.
- No survey instrument has been published studying workplace experiences of
  women, Black, Hispanic, or other minority land surveyors.
- CONTRAST: Architecture has the AIA Workplace Experiences Survey and the
  NCARB/NOMA Joint Survey; construction has the JME special collection.

GAP 4 (MAJOR): SURVEYING EDUCATION PIPELINE NOT SYSTEMATICALLY TRACKED
- Anecdotal reports of program closures but no comprehensive time series of
  ABET-accredited surveying program enrollments, graduates, or program counts.
- No study examines the demographic composition of surveying students.
- CONTRAST: Engineering broadly has ASEE annual data; architecture has NAAB
  enrollment data.

GAP 5 (MODERATE): NO ECONOMIC IMPACT STUDY OF THE SURVEYING SHORTAGE
- No study quantifies the economic impact of surveying workforce shortages
  on construction timelines, real estate transactions, or infrastructure
  delivery.
- PropLogix and trade sources mention project delays anecdotally.

GAP 6 (MODERATE): CROSS-INDUSTRY COMPARISON FRAMEWORK DOES NOT EXIST
- No published study compares surveying workforce demographics and pipeline
  to peer professions (civil engineering, architecture, GIS, construction).
- This would be a significant original contribution.

GAP 7 (MODERATE): APPRENTICESHIP AND ALTERNATIVE PATHWAY OUTCOMES NOT STUDIED
- SAM apprenticeship and a few other programs documented, but no peer-reviewed
  evaluation of outcomes (completion rates, licensure rates, retention,
  demographic composition).
- CONTRAST: DOL publishes apprenticeship completion data for plumbing and
  electrical trades.

GAP 8 (MINOR): TECHNOLOGY DISPLACEMENT EFFECTS ON SURVEYING WORKFORCE
NOT EMPIRICALLY MEASURED
- BLS notes that drones/technology "may limit growth" but no study quantifies
  how many positions have been eliminated or created by technology adoption.
- GeoAI projections (Project Geospatial 2025) are forward-looking estimates,
  not empirical measurement.

SECTION 3: LITERATURE SPARSITY COMPARISON
================================================================================

Ranking of peer-reviewed DEI/workforce literature volume by field:

1. CIVIL ENGINEERING / CONSTRUCTION - EXTENSIVE
   - Multiple special issues, systematic reviews, and individual studies
   - 23+ papers in JME special collection alone
   - Regular workforce surveys from AGC, NCCER, Harvard JCHS

2. ARCHITECTURE - SUBSTANTIAL
   - Annual NCARB demographic data; AIA research program
   - NCARB/NOMA joint survey on minority experience
   - Regular tracking of diversity progress in licensure pipeline

3. GEOSCIENCES (broadly) - MODERATE
   - AGI biennial workforce reports
   - Bezdek (2009) and follow-up projections
   - USGS and federal agency workforce data

4. PLUMBING/ELECTRICAL TRADES - LIMITED BUT GROWING
   - DOL apprenticeship data provides baseline
   - Emerging DEI program evaluations
   - Mental health/retention research appearing

5. GIS/REMOTE SENSING - LIMITED
   - Global market reports but sparse demographic data
   - Skills mismatch discussed in trade press
   - No systematic workforce demographics study found

6. LAND SURVEYING - VERY SPARSE (<<< THIS IS YOUR FIELD)
   - ONE peer-reviewed paper on regulation/accreditation (2025)
   - ONE non-peer-reviewed analysis on Academia.edu
   - ONE trade organization survey (NSPS)
   - Heavy reliance on BLS projections and trade press
   - NO published DEI study specific to surveying
   - NO longitudinal workforce tracking

CONCLUSION: Land surveying has the LEAST developed academic literature on
workforce demographics and diversity of any adjacent field examined. This
represents both a gap and an opportunity for original contribution. The three-
paper series has a strong rationale: the literature simply does not exist yet.
