# Literature Scan: Consolidated Gap Analysis Report

**Surveying Workforce Research Series**
**Date:** 2026-03-28
**Queries executed:** 29 across three topic areas
**Sources cataloged:** ~97 across all scans

This report synthesizes findings from three parallel literature scans:
- Workforce aging & cross-industry comparisons (30 sources, 11 queries)
- Demographic disparity (35 sources, 7+ queries)
- Pipeline solutions & program models (32 sources, 11 queries)

## Section 1: What Already Exists

### Surveying-Specific Literature

The peer-reviewed literature on the U.S. land surveying workforce is **near-empty**:

- **ONE peer-reviewed paper**: Wiejak-Roy & Edwards (2025), "Trends and challenges
  in regulation and accreditation of land surveying professions in the United States
  of America and the United Kingdom," *Survey Review*. Focused on regulation, not
  workforce demographics. [web-verified]

- **ONE working paper**: Anonymous, "Surveying Shortage: A Brief Look at the
  Evidence," Academia.edu. Not peer-reviewed, undated, author unclear.
  [needs manual verification]

- **ONE trade organization survey**: NSPS Demographics Survey (n=2,409). Results:
  94.3% male, 92.3% White, 0.3% Black (8 respondents). Undated, convenience sample,
  not peer-reviewed. [web-verified: https://cdn.ymaws.com/nsps.us.com/resource/resmgr/workforce_development/Surveying_Profession_Demogra.pdf]

**Critical observation:** The raw data infrastructure exists (ACS PUMS, EEO Tabulation,
BLS). The methods exist in adjacent-field studies. **No scholar has applied them to
surveying.** This is the central finding of this literature scan.

### Adjacent-Field Literature (rich by comparison)

**Civil engineering / construction:**
- ASCE JME published 23+ papers in a 2022 special collection on DEI
- ASCE JPIEP published a 2018 special issue on diversity
- Heydari et al. (2024): systematic review on DEI in AEC industry
- AGC/NCCER: 92% of firms report hiring difficulty; 41% retiring by 2031

**Architecture:**
- NCARB "By the Numbers" (2024): 34% of new architects are people of color
  (up from 25% in 2019); Black architects still ~2% of practitioners

**Geosciences:**
- AGI: 27% workforce loss by 2029 from retirements
- Regular biennial workforce reports

**Trades (plumbing/electrical):**
- ICERES/NABTU (2023): union apprenticeships outperform non-union on diversity
  (women completion 37.6% vs 23.4%)

## Section 2: What Does NOT Exist (The Gaps)

### Gap 1: No peer-reviewed demographic analysis of surveyors using Census/ACS data
- **What is missing:** Nobody has used ACS PUMS microdata to analyze the demographic
  composition of the surveying workforce by race, ethnicity, sex, age, education,
  veteran status, or geography.
- **Why it matters:** ACS PUMS is the gold standard for occupation-level workforce
  analysis. Every adjacent field has published such analyses. Surveying has zero.
- **Which paper fills it:** Paper 2 (primary), Paper 1 (age analysis)
- **Originality strength:** **STRONG** — first-of-its-kind, no competition

### Gap 2: No longitudinal workforce tracking for surveying
- **What is missing:** No study tracks surveyor workforce size, age distribution,
  or demographic composition over time.
- **Why it matters:** Cannot quantify the retirement cliff or measure whether the
  profession is diversifying without trend data.
- **Which paper fills it:** Paper 1
- **Originality strength:** **STRONG**

### Gap 3: No state-level licensure trend data compiled
- **What is missing:** Anecdotal reports of licensure decline are abundant, but no
  study aggregates PLS/RPLS counts across states over time.
- **Why it matters:** State-level data would show where the crisis is most acute
  and whether any states are bucking the trend.
- **Which paper fills it:** Paper 1
- **Originality strength:** **STRONG** (if data can be obtained from state boards)

### Gap 4: No cross-profession comparison using consistent data
- **What is missing:** Nobody has compared surveying's demographics to civil
  engineering, architecture, construction, and GIS using the same ACS data source.
- **Why it matters:** Shows how surveying ranks among peer professions on diversity.
- **Which paper fills it:** Paper 2
- **Originality strength:** **STRONG**

### Gap 5: No intersectional analysis (race x gender x age x geography)
- **What is missing:** No study examines how race and gender interact in the
  surveying workforce, or how demographics vary by state/metro area.
- **Why it matters:** Women of color are likely the most underrepresented subgroup;
  quantifying this is essential for targeted pipeline programs.
- **Which paper fills it:** Paper 2
- **Originality strength:** **MODERATE-STRONG** (sample size dependent)

### Gap 6: No published evaluation of any surveying pipeline program
- **What is missing:** Klein Collins, SC Survey JAC, Bolton & Menk, community
  college programs — none have published outcome evaluations (completion rates,
  licensure rates, demographic composition, employment outcomes).
- **Why it matters:** Cannot scale what you cannot measure.
- **Which paper fills it:** Paper 3 (documents the gap; proposes evaluation framework)
- **Originality strength:** **MODERATE** (inventory/framework contribution, not empirical)

### Gap 7: No military-to-civilian surveying pipeline despite MOS 12T
- **What is missing:** MOS 12T provides directly transferable surveying skills.
  No SkillBridge partnerships exist. No data on 12T-to-civilian-surveying transition.
- **Why it matters:** Veterans are an underutilized, already-trained talent pool.
- **Which paper fills it:** Paper 3
- **Originality strength:** **STRONG** (specific, actionable, novel)

### Gap 8: Only one HBCU offers surveying/geomatics
- **What is missing:** NC A&T is the sole HBCU with an ABET-accredited geomatics
  program. No other HBCUs or MSIs have been identified.
- **Why it matters:** A single institution cannot serve as the pipeline for an
  entire underrepresented demographic.
- **Which paper fills it:** Paper 3
- **Originality strength:** **MODERATE** (documentation contribution)

### Gap 9: NSPS does not publish member demographics
- **What is missing:** The national professional association conducted one survey
  but does not publish annual demographic data as NCARB and AIA do.
- **Why it matters:** Without baseline data, the profession cannot measure progress.
- **Which paper fills it:** Papers 2 and 3 (the absence is itself a finding)
- **Originality strength:** **MODERATE** (critique/recommendation)

### Gap 10: Racial/ethnic diversity in surveying is essentially unstudied
- **What is missing:** Trade articles discuss "women in surveying" occasionally.
  Racial/ethnic representation gets almost no coverage.
- **Why it matters:** Black representation at 0.3% (NSPS) to 3.55% (BLS) in a
  profession critical to infrastructure is a significant equity issue.
- **Which paper fills it:** Paper 2
- **Originality strength:** **STRONG**

## Section 3: Recommended Paper Sequencing and Journal Targeting

### Recommended sequence: Paper 2 first, then Paper 1, then Paper 3

**Rationale:**

1. **Paper 2 has the strongest originality claim.** Zero prior work exists on
   surveyor demographics using Census/ACS data. The contribution is clear,
   defensible, and requires no external data beyond IPUMS. Reviewers will
   immediately see this fills a gap because the literature review can cite
   extensive adjacent-field work and show surveying has nothing comparable.

2. **Paper 1 benefits from Paper 2's foundation.** The aging analysis is stronger
   when contextualized by who is aging out (overwhelmingly White men) and who
   is not entering (everyone else). Paper 2 provides that context. Paper 1 can
   cite Paper 2 (submitted or under review) to strengthen its argument.

3. **Paper 3 requires Papers 1 and 2 as its evidence base.** The pipeline solutions
   argument is strongest when it can cite specific numbers: "X% of surveyors are
   over 55, only Y% are non-White, therefore pipeline programs must target Z
   populations." Without Papers 1 and 2, Paper 3 is a practitioner essay rather
   than a research-grounded policy paper.

### Journal targeting:

| Paper | Primary Target | Secondary Target | Rationale |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|-----------|
| Paper 2 | ASCE J. Surveying Engineering | ASCE JPIEP | JSE is the most directly relevant journal; JPIEP has published similar DEI work for civil engineering |
| Paper 1 | ASCE J. Surveying Engineering | Surveying and Land Information Science (SALIS) | JSE for maximum prestige; SALIS for practitioner reach |
| Paper 3 | ASCE JPIEP | SALIS or Professional Surveyor | JPIEP has published pipeline/education research; SALIS for practitioner audience |

**Note on ASCE J. Surveying Engineering:** This is the most prestigious peer-reviewed
journal specific to surveying. Publishing Papers 1 and 2 there would establish the
research program in the field's top venue. However, JSE has historically focused on
technical/geodetic topics rather than workforce issues. The workforce angle may be
novel enough to interest editors, but JPIEP is a safer bet for the DEI framing.

**Recommendation:** Submit Paper 2 to ASCE JPIEP (which has published civil engineering
DEI work) and Paper 1 to ASCE JSE (workforce aging is closer to a policy/planning
topic the journal may accept). Paper 3 to SALIS for maximum practitioner impact.

## Section 4: Proactive Suggestions

The following suggestions emerged from the literature scan. Each would strengthen
the research series. User should confirm, modify, or decline each one.

### Suggestion 1: OLS regression for wage gaps (Paper 2)
Run log(hourly_wage) regression on race_eth + sex_r + educ_r + agegroup + veteran
+ classwkr_r + state FE within surveyors. Coefficients on race/sex after controls
are the clearest evidence of unexplained wage gaps. Adjacent-field studies (Shrestha
et al. 2022) use this approach. **Substantially strengthens peer review credibility.**

### Suggestion 2: ACS 1-year trend analysis (Paper 1)
Pull ACS 1-year data for surveyors across 2010-2023 to show age distribution shift
over time. If median age has risen 5+ years in a decade, that is a powerful standalone
finding not available from the 5-year pooled data alone.

### Suggestion 3: Architecture as the comparison model (Papers 2 and 3)
Architecture's progress (34% of new architects are people of color, up from 25% in
2019) is directly attributable to intentional programs by NCARB, NOMA, and AIA.
Use architecture as the primary comparison case: "here is what a profession looks
like when it invests in diversity infrastructure, and here is what surveying looks
like without it."

### Suggestion 4: NSPS demographic reporting gap as a finding (Papers 2 and 3)
The fact that NSPS does not publish annual membership demographics — while NCARB,
AIA, and ASCE do — is itself evidence of institutional inattention to diversity.
Frame this explicitly as a finding and recommendation, not just a limitation.

### Suggestion 5: DoD SkillBridge + MOS 12T as an actionable pipeline (Paper 3)
No surveying-specific SkillBridge partnership exists despite MOS 12T providing
directly transferable skills. This is a specific, novel, and immediately actionable
recommendation. If even one firm or state society establishes a SkillBridge
partnership as a result of this paper, that is real-world impact.

### Suggestion 6: International comparison (Paper 2 or standalone)
The UK (RICS data: 16% female Chartered Surveyors, up from 12% in 2012) and
Australia track surveyor demographics. A brief international comparison would add
a comparative dimension reviewers respond well to and show the US is behind.

### Suggestion 7: Disability representation analysis (Paper 2)
If DIFFREM/DIFFEYE/DIFFPHYS variables are included in the IPUMS extract, analyze
disability representation in surveying vs. STEM benchmarks. This adds an additional
underrepresented group dimension that no prior work has examined.

### Suggestion 8: Self-employment rates by race/ethnicity (Paper 2)
If minority surveyors are disproportionately self-employed, that could reflect either
entrepreneurship or structural exclusion from larger firms. Worth testing.
