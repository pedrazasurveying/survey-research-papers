## 2. CRITICAL GAPS IDENTIFIED

### GAP 1: NO peer-reviewed paper has used ACS/Census PUMS microdata to analyze surveyor-specific demographics by race, ethnicity, and gender.
- **Significance:** HIGH. The data exists (ACS PUMS occupation codes for surveyors, cartographers, photogrammetrists), the EEO Tabulation provides pre-tabulated breakdowns, and IPUMS makes the microdata accessible — but NO scholar has published an analysis focused on surveying workforce demographics using this data.
- **Comparison:** Civil engineering has multiple published studies (Shrestha et al., Pearson & Simmons, ASCE Demographic Profile Report). Architecture has similar studies. Surveying has zero.
- **Opportunity:** Paper 2 can be the FIRST to conduct this analysis.

### GAP 2: No longitudinal analysis of demographic change in surveying workforce over time.
- **Significance:** HIGH. BLS CPS data goes back decades and ACS PUMS data from 2005. Nobody has tracked how the racial/gender composition of surveyors has changed over 10-20 years, even as adjacent professions have published such analyses.
- **Opportunity:** Paper 2 can construct time series from ACS PUMS (2005-2024) or CPS data.

### GAP 3: No intersectional analysis (race x gender x age x geography) for surveyors.
- **Significance:** MEDIUM-HIGH. Martinez & Christnacht (2019) did this for broad STEM. Reskin (1996) showed the methodology. Nobody has done it for surveying. The NSPS survey had too few non-White/non-male respondents to support intersectional analysis.
- **Opportunity:** ACS PUMS pooled 5-year samples may provide enough observations for basic intersectional breakdowns.

### GAP 4: No comparison of surveying's demographic profile to adjacent professions using the same data source.
- **Significance:** MEDIUM-HIGH. How does surveying compare to civil engineering, architecture, construction management, and GIS/cartography on race/gender composition? Nobody has done this comparison systematically using consistent data.
- **Opportunity:** Paper 2 can pull multiple occupation codes from ACS PUMS and compare.

### GAP 5: No analysis of geographic variation in surveyor demographics.
- **Significance:** MEDIUM. The RPLS.com forum discussion noted anecdotally that some states have zero non-White surveyors. The EEO Tabulation provides state- and metro-level data. Nobody has mapped this.
- **Opportunity:** Paper 2 can use EEO tabulation or ACS PUMS (with geographic identifiers) to map diversity deserts.

### GAP 6: No analysis linking demographic composition to workforce shortage/pipeline.
- **Significance:** MEDIUM. The workforce shortage literature (Paper 1 territory) and the demographics literature exist in separate silos. Nobody has asked: "To what extent is the surveying workforce shortage driven by or exacerbated by the failure to recruit from non-White and female populations?"
- **Opportunity:** Paper 2 can quantify the untapped labor pool, or Paper 3 can frame pipeline solutions in terms of demographic expansion.

### GAP 7: The NSPS survey is the only surveyor-specific demographic dataset, and it has severe limitations.
- **Significance:** HIGH. Convenience sample of NSPS members; no year published; likely skews older/White/male because of who joins NSPS; only 8 Black respondents out of 2,409 (0.3%) vs BLS showing 3.55% Black in the occupation.
- **Opportunity:** ACS PUMS provides a probability-based, nationally representative alternative.

### GAP 8: Almost no attention to racial/ethnic diversity in surveying — gender gets some coverage, race gets almost none.
- **Significance:** HIGH. Several trade articles discuss "women in surveying." Almost nothing discusses Black, Hispanic, Native American, or Asian representation in surveying. The NSPS survey collected the data but it has not been analyzed or discussed in any published work found.
- **Opportunity:** Paper 2 can foreground racial/ethnic analysis alongside gender.

## 4. KEY DATA SOURCES FOR PAPER 2

### Primary (recommended)
1. **ACS PUMS via IPUMS** — Occupation codes for surveyors, cartographers, photogrammetrists. Variables: SEX, RAC1P, HISP, AGEP, SCHL, WAGP, OCCP, STATEFIP, PUMA. Pool 5-year samples for adequate sample sizes. Available 2005-2024.
2. **Census EEO Tabulation 2014-2018** — Pre-tabulated race/sex by detailed occupation at multiple geographic levels. Table EEOALL01R or EEOALL01W.

### Secondary/Comparison
3. **BLS CPS Table 11** — Annual race/sex by detailed occupation. Good for trends but small sample sizes for rare occupations.
4. **NSPS Demographics Survey** — Useful as practitioner-reported comparison to Census data.
5. **ASEE data** — Surveying/geomatics program enrollment demographics for pipeline analysis.

### Methodological Models from Adjacent Literature
- Pew Research (2021) methodology: IPUMS ACS microdata, occupation-cluster analysis, trend analysis
- Reskin (1996): Duncan Segregation Index applied to PUMS occupation data
- Landivar (2013): ACS Reports format for occupation-level demographic analysis
- Martinez & Christnacht (2019): Intersectionality framework applied to ACS STEM data

## 5. SEARCH-BY-SEARCH SUMMARY

### Q1: "land surveying diversity equity inclusion"
- **Found:** Corporate DEI pages (BKF Engineers); USDA/USGS institutional pages; land trust DEI reports. NO academic research on DEI in land surveying specifically.
- **Gap:** Term "DEI" in surveying context returns corporate/institutional pages, not scholarship.

### Q2: "surveying profession demographics race gender"
- **Found:** BLS CPS Table 11; Zippia/CareerExplorer aggregations; RICS/UK gender data; NSPS survey.
- **Gap:** Data exists but no published academic analysis of it.

### Q3: "geomatics workforce diversity"
- **Found:** WGIC report (42% female in GIS); GoGeomatics Canada crisis article; GIM International education reform; UN-GGIM diversity side event; GCAP workforce development.
- **Gap:** Geomatics/GIS literature discusses gender more than race; no peer-reviewed diversity studies for geomatics workforce.

### Q4: "ACS American Community Survey surveyor occupation demographics"
- **Found:** ACS documentation; PUMS access tools; EEO Tabulation documentation. Data infrastructure exists.
- **Gap:** Nobody has published an analysis using ACS data for surveyor demographics. The data is there; the scholarship is not.

### Q5: "STEM workforce diversity race ethnicity ACS microdata"
- **Found:** Rich literature — Pew (2021), NSF NCSES (2021, 2024), Census ACS-24 (2013), Martinez & Christnacht (2019). All use ACS microdata.
- **Gap:** All analyze broad STEM or occupation clusters. None disaggregate to surveyor level. Surveying falls through the cracks — too small for cluster-level analysis, not separately reported in any published study.

### Q6: "women in surveying"
- **Found:** Multiple trade articles; PDH Academy blog; firm websites; LinkedIn article on RICS data; historical profiles (Alice Fletcher, Irene Barclay).
- **Gap:** No peer-reviewed study on women in surveying. Exclusively trade/opinion pieces. Compare to civil engineering which has peer-reviewed retention studies.

### Q7: "minority representation technical professions land surveying"
- **Found:** RPLS.com forum discussion; Survey Review (2025) peer-reviewed article on regulation; Academia.edu working paper on shortage; BLS occupation profiles.
- **Gap:** Almost nothing on racial/ethnic minority representation in surveying specifically. The topic appears essentially unstudied.

---

## 6. BOTTOM LINE

The literature scan reveals a near-complete absence of peer-reviewed research on demographic diversity in the U.S. land surveying profession. While adjacent fields (civil engineering, architecture, construction) have growing bodies of DEI scholarship using Census/ACS data, and while the broader STEM diversity literature uses ACS PUMS microdata extensively, NO published study has applied these methods or data to surveyors specifically. The raw data exists in ACS PUMS and the EEO Tabulation. The methodological templates exist in adjacent-field studies. The gap is entirely in the surveying-specific application. This represents a significant and clearly definable contribution opportunity for Paper 2 of the research series.
