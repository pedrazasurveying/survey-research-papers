# Pipeline Solutions Literature Scan: Gap Notes

## Date: 2026-03-28
## Scope: 11 queries across CTE, apprenticeship, community college, HBCU, veteran, diversity, and workforce development programs related to U.S. land surveying

---

## What Programs Exist

**High School / CTE:**
- Texas leads with a statewide CTE program of study in Geospatial Engineering & Land Surveying, requiring regional labor market approval. Klein ISD (Klein Collins HS) operates the first Texas high school pathway of its kind, offering dual credit through Lone Star College, an AS in GIS, and industry certifications (AutoCAD, NSPS CST I, FAA drone pilot). Virginia also has a geospatial technology CTE pathway.

**Apprenticeship:**
- Several registered apprenticeship programs exist: Southern California Surveyors JAC (3-year, Chainman/Chief of Party tracks), Technical Engineers Apprenticeship (California), Bolton & Menk (Iowa/North Carolina, 2-year), and NJSPLS (New Jersey). Apprenticeship.gov lists surveying under two occupation codes. The SC Survey JAC explicitly recruits veterans.

**Community College:**
- At least 8-10 community colleges offer surveying/geomatics AAS degrees or certificates (Cleveland State CC, SCTCC, KCKCC, Wake Tech, FTCC, Cape Fear CC, TCC, CCBC, Sinclair, Salt Lake CC). Several provide transfer pathways to 4-year programs. California community colleges prepare students for state licensing exams.

**HBCU:**
- North Carolina A&T is the only identified HBCU with an ABET-accredited 4-year geomatics/surveying program. It reports 80-90% placement rates and ~$70K starting salaries. It received NCEES Surveying Education Awards and was ranked nationally for its online GIS program.

**Veteran/Military Transition:**
- MOS 12T (Technical Engineer Specialist) is the closest military occupational specialty to civilian surveying. Training includes GPS, CAD, geodetic surveys, and topographic mapping. The SC Survey JAC recruits veterans with GI Bill. However, no surveying-specific SkillBridge partnerships were identified.

**Geospatial Workforce Development:**
- The National Geospatial Innovation Hub Advancement Act (2024, in FY25 NDAA) authorized a 5-year pilot program with $5M for UMSL in partnership with NGA. This is defense/intelligence focused.

**Diversity Pipeline (Adjacent Fields):**
- ASCE runs DEI initiatives through its MOSAIC committee and hosted the first JEDI Workforce Summit. The CEDAP program ($5M federal grant, Contra Costa County) targets underrepresented groups for civil engineering pathways. ICERES/NABTU research shows union apprenticeship programs produce greater diversity than non-union programs. Pre-apprenticeship programs with wrap-around services are identified as effective.

**Scholarships/Awareness:**
- NSPS Foundation distributes ~$97K annually; total with state societies ~$350K/year. Trig-Star is a high school math competition designed to introduce students to surveying.

---

## What Evaluations Have Been Published

Very few rigorous evaluations exist:

1. **ICERES/NABTU (2023)** produced the most data-driven analysis, showing union apprenticeships outperform non-union on diversity metrics (women completion: 37.6% vs. 23.4%). However, this covers all construction trades, not surveying specifically.

2. **NC A&T** reports placement rates (80-90%) and starting salaries (~$70K) but these are self-reported institutional data, not independent evaluations.

3. **ASCE/NGA (2023)** convening produced strategic recommendations but no program evaluations.

4. **PMC qualitative study (2023)** on veteran career transitions (n=15) is academic but not surveying-specific.

5. **No published program evaluations** were found for any surveying apprenticeship, CTE program, or community college program. Klein Collins, SC Survey JAC, Bolton & Menk, and all community college programs lack publicly available outcome data.

---

## Key Gaps

1. **No surveying-specific diversity data.** While adjacent fields (construction trades, civil engineering) have some diversity research, no study breaks out the surveying/geomatics profession specifically by race, gender, or socioeconomic status within pipeline programs.

2. **No program evaluation research.** None of the identified surveying pipeline programs (apprenticeships, CTE, community college) have published rigorous evaluations of completion rates, licensure rates, employment outcomes, or return on investment.

3. **Only one HBCU.** NC A&T appears to be the sole HBCU with a dedicated geomatics program. The absence of other HBCUs represents a critical pipeline gap for Black surveyors.

4. **Military-to-civilian transition gap.** Despite MOS 12T providing directly transferable surveying skills, no formal SkillBridge-to-surveying pipeline exists. No data on how many 12T veterans enter civilian surveying careers.

5. **Scholarship impact unmeasured.** NSPS distributes ~$350K/year in scholarships but no data exists on whether recipients complete programs, obtain licensure, or remain in the profession.

6. **Geographic concentration.** Identified programs cluster in Texas, California, North Carolina, and a few other states. Large regions of the country appear to lack accessible surveying education pathways.

7. **No research on what attracts diverse candidates.** While construction trades research identifies pre-apprenticeship programs and wrap-around services as effective, no equivalent research exists for surveying.

8. **Geospatial workforce programs focus on intelligence, not surveying.** The UMSL/NGA pilot and related legislation target geospatial intelligence careers, not professional land surveying licensure.

9. **Community college to licensure pipeline is opaque.** Multiple community colleges offer programs, but data on how many graduates actually achieve professional licensure is not tracked or published.

10. **No longitudinal studies.** No research tracks surveying students from entry into a pipeline program through licensure and career retention.
